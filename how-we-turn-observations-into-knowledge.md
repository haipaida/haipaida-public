# How We Turn Observations Into Knowledge

At Hyperping Solutions, we believe useful network knowledge should come from real observations, testing, and continuous learning.

## From Problems To Knowledge

Many connectivity problems appear simple at first.

However, the actual cause may come from different areas:

- Local network conditions
- ISP routing
- Cross-region paths
- Server locations
- Device or environment limitations

Before reaching a conclusion, we first try to understand the complete situation.

Our general process is:

1. Understand the reported problem.
2. Collect relevant environment information.
3. Observe and test possible causes.
4. Compare findings over time.
5. Document the results.
6. Extract lessons that may help similar situations.

## How We Share Knowledge

Based on our observations, we maintain several types of public resources.

### Real Cases

Real cases document specific situations and investigation results.

They help explain why similar problems may happen and what factors should be considered.

### Route References

Route references record observations from different regions, networks, and destinations.

They are intended as references, not guarantees, because network conditions can change.

### Technical Notes

Technical notes explain common connectivity concepts and troubleshooting ideas.

Their purpose is to help users understand problems before making changes.

## What We Avoid

We avoid:

- Treating one solution as suitable for every situation.
- Making conclusions from a single measurement.
- Recommending changes without understanding the cause.
- Confusing temporary improvements with long-term stability.

## Continuous Learning

Networks are constantly changing.

Public documentation is not a final answer. It is a record of observations, testing, evaluation, and lessons learned over time.

By documenting what we observe, we can continue improving our understanding of connectivity.
