# Why Stable Latency Matters More Than The Lowest Ping

A lower ping number is usually desirable.

But the lowest ping does not always provide the best connection.

For real-time applications, consistency often matters just as much as raw latency.

## Low Ping Can Still Feel Unstable

A connection may show a relatively low average ping while still experiencing:

* Sudden latency spikes
* Jitter
* Packet loss
* Short periods of congestion
* Route changes
* Uneven performance over time

These variations can make a connection feel worse than another route with slightly higher but more consistent latency.

## Stability Makes Performance Predictable

A stable connection behaves more consistently from one moment to the next.

For example, a route that stays near 80 ms may provide a better experience than one that moves repeatedly between 50 ms and 140 ms.

The second route may occasionally produce a lower number, but its behaviour is less predictable.

## We Look Beyond A Single Number

When evaluating a route, we do not only ask:

> What is the lowest ping?

We also look at:

* How much latency changes over time
* Whether packet loss appears
* Whether performance changes during peak hours
* Whether the route remains consistent
* Whether the same behaviour can be reproduced

A single good result does not necessarily represent long-term performance.

## Lower Is Better Only When Stability Is Preserved

Reducing latency is useful when the improvement remains stable.

If a route becomes slightly faster but introduces more jitter, packet loss, or inconsistency, the change may not be an improvement overall.

This is why route evaluation should consider both latency and stability.

## The Goal Is Consistent Performance

We do not try to optimize a connection only for the smallest possible ping number.

The goal is to find a route that performs predictably under real conditions.

In many situations, stable latency provides more useful performance than occasional low latency.
